The problem

Too many children start school without the basics

In many government schools in India, children move past Class 1 without being able to read a simple sentence or do simple sums. Once they fall behind, most never catch up.

Research has already shown what works: teaching at each child's level, in the language they speak at home, with plenty of practice. The challenge is doing all of it together, every day, in every school, at a price a state government can afford.

Our answer

One hour a day, built on what schools already have

  • A daily 60-minute block for Class 1, run by the class teacher.
  • Lessons and books in the child's mother tongue.
  • Uses the teacher tablets, teachers and mentors the government already has.
  • Needs no internet and no teaching time from parents.
  • Designed to run across a whole district.

How it works

A school day with Samjho, Phir Abhyas Karo

Each new skill moves from understanding to practice, and no child is left stuck.

Understand

A short, interactive audio lesson is played from the teacher's existing tablet, with cards, counters and other hands-on materials.

Practise

Children practise in pairs on shared tablets, about one for every ten children. The app works offline and creates unlimited questions at each child's level.

Get help

Older students from Classes 4 and 5 tutor using level cards, and the teacher works with the small group that is stuck that day.

Track

Weekly checks on practice and monthly progress checks show schools and block coordinators who needs more support.

Evidence

Every part has worked in randomised trials

The model brings together four approaches that have each worked in rigorous studies in low- and middle-income countries.

ComponentWhere it was testedEffect
Teaching at the child's level in learning campsUttar Pradesh, India (Banerjee et al., 2016)+0.70 SD
Structured literacy in the mother tongueUganda (Kerwin and Thornton, 2021)+0.64 SD
Interactive audio instructionBangladesh (Wang et al., 2024)+0.60 SD
Shared offline adaptive devicesKenya (EdTech Hub)+0.53 SD

SD = standard deviations of test scores. Results from one setting do not guarantee the same results elsewhere, which is why the model will first be tested in a small number of schools.
